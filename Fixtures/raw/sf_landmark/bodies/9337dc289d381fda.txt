                <div lang="en" class="field__item"><p>The Landmark Tree Program&nbsp;aims to acknowledge, celebrate, and protect trees in San Francisco that have environmental, cultural, historical, botanical, or other significance. The program was codified in 2006 in Article 16, Section 810 of the&nbsp;<a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_publicworks/0-0-0-4204">Public Works Code</a>.</p>

                    <div lang="en" class="field__item"><p data-start="195" data-end="530">San Francisco’s Landmark Trees are recognized for their environmental, cultural, historical, botanical, and community significance. The Landmark Tree Program honors exceptional trees across the city and provides a formal process for identifying and protecting those that make a lasting contribution to San Francisco’s urban forest.</p><p data-start="535" data-end="744">Below you’ll find an <strong data-start="556" data-end="575">interactive map</strong> of designated landmark trees, <strong data-start="606" data-end="636">frequently asked questions</strong> about the program and nomination criteria, and the <strong data-start="688" data-end="723">forms needed to nominate a tree</strong> for landmark status.</p><p>&nbsp;</p><h2 class="text-align-center">Landmark Tree Map</h2><p class="text-align-center"><iframe height="480" src="https://www.google.com/maps/d/embed?mid=1NK4oc4n35UXpAt19FrJUHAkQaxmvmv9a" width="640"></iframe></p><p>&nbsp;</p></div>

                    <div lang="en" class="field__item"><h2>Background and process</h2>
<p>Before 2006, San Francisco Public Works designated landmark trees under its jurisdiction through an internal process. The adoption of the Landmark Tree Ordinance streamlined this approach and expanded who can nominate trees, allowing eligible parties to propose landmark trees anywhere in San Francisco.</p>

      How the review process works
      <svg class="arrow" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="24" height="24" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="#000000" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><path d="M6 9l6 6 6-6"></path></svg>

<div lang="en" class="field--type-entity-reference-revisions field--name-field-contents">

    <div lang="en" class="field--type-text-long field--name-field-full-html text-formatted clearfix">
        <div class="field__items">
                    <div lang="en" class="field__item"><ul>
<li>The Urban Forestry Council oversees the nomination process and develops evaluation criteria.</li>
<li>Each nomination is reviewed during at least two public meetings held by the Urban Forestry Council.</li>
<li>The Urban Forestry Council makes a recommendation to the Board of Supervisors for each application.</li>

    <div lang="en" class="field--type-text-long field--name-field-full-html text-formatted clearfix">
        <div class="field__items">
                    <div lang="en" class="field__item"><p>Given the density of San Francisco's built environment, trees sometimes contribute to disagreements between neighbors and property owners. Sometimes this involves a truly magnificent tree specimen, but not always. The Landmark Tree Ordinance is meant to be used proactively to highlight important individual trees throughout San Francisco. The program is not intended to be used reactively by property owners or others to prevent a tree being removed or to gain advantage in a neighborhood dispute. In fact, San Francisco has a <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_publicworks/0-0-0-4287">Tree Dispute Resolution ordinance</a>, the purpose of which is to provide a process for neighboring property owners to settle their differences if they could not otherwise find a just resolution.</p>

          <details class="js-form-wrapper form-wrapper"><summary role="button" aria-expanded="false" class="notranslate">
      Can I nominate a tree in my backyard if the trunk straddles the property line with my neighbor? 
      <svg class="arrow" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="24" height="24" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="#000000" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><path d="M6 9l6 6 6-6"></path></svg>
    </summary><div class="details-wrapper">
    
<div lang="en" class="field--type-entity-reference-revisions field--name-field-contents">
              <div class="field__items">
              <div class="field__item">  <div id="¶-12418" class="¶--type-full-html display-contents">
    <div lang="en" class="field--type-text-long field--name-field-full-html text-formatted clearfix">
        <div class="field__items">
                    <div lang="en" class="field__item"><p>Yes, but only if the other property owner co-nominates the tree. Under California Civil Code Sections 833 and 834, tree ownership is defined by where a tree trunk&nbsp;stands. For the purposes of Public Works Code Section 810(b) regarding nominations of trees for landmark status, a property "contains" a tree if its trunk is within the property line, regardless of where its roots or branches may extend. If a tree trunk is wholly on the land of one owner, then the tree belongs exclusively to that&nbsp;owner, and the owner's property "contains" the tree; if it straddles the properties of two owners, then it belongs to them in common, and both properties "contain" the tree for purposes of tree nomination. If your tree trunk (not roots or branches) breaks the soil surface on both sides of the line, then co-nomination is required.</p>
</div>
